## Workshop

Practice as Research Workshop of Diffraction Theatre have explored how theatrical practice can be used as a tool for the critique of digital technology, exploring the pressing issues raised by digital technology in everyday life through audience participation. This workshop explored dramaturgy, scenography, spectatorship, and the body as a sensor-alongside digital sensors- through AI teddy bear. It acted as a form of green theatre, mixing material and discursive practices, and offers a space where theory and practice meet-linking seminars, rehearsals, and education.

The workshop included three sessions: Half hour introduction, three-hour session, and a 30-minute break. Participants have engaged in hands-on sitespecific theatrical exercises to critically explore hidden sensors and digital data collection embedded in everyday objects-such as toys, smart home items. Together, we have examined how surveillance capitalism operates through these ordinary items, turning human behavior into data for prediction and profit. Through this process, participants have discovered how theatre can be used as a powerful tool to question and reflect on the role of digital technologies in our lives.

## What is Practice as Research (PaR)?

- It refers to a research methodology in which artistic practice itself constitutes the research.
- The creative process, the resulting artwork, and critical reflection on that practice collectively generate new knowledge.
- This knowledge is not a collection of texts, but rather an organized system that can be roamed, combined, and performed.
- Transforming abstract theory into tangible material for practice and research
- In this approach, practice is not merely a tool or means to an end but is integral to the research inquiry. (Barrett & Bolt, 2014; Biggs et al., 2011; Nelson, 2022).

## What is Surveillance Capitalism?

Surveillance capitalism unliterally claims human experience as free raw material for translation into behavioural data. Although some of these data are applied to service improvement, the rest are declared as a proprietary behavioural surplus, fed into advanced manufacturing processes known as “machine intelligence”, and fabricated into prediction products that anticipate what you will do now, soon, and later. (Shoshana Zuboff, 2019, p8)

After behavioural surplus was discovered, surveillance capitalism was
born. Behavioural data, currently preserved for conversion, far exceeds
the data needs for improving service quality. This surplus has become the
fodder for machine intelligence, with behavioural prediction products
built from this new production method.

## Scenography

Materials: Stage-model materials from the model kit.

### Steps

- Build a space scenography according to the reference image.
- Place everyday household items in the space.
- Label which items may contain sensors for data collection.
- Who will live in this space? Place figurines in the space.

Scenography is an art form that drives emotional responses through the
arrangement of numerous elements such as space, matter,
sound, and the body. Scenography is not just about seeing, but also about
perception (JWT, 2026).

Stage models as an active agent: How do stage models work as epistemic tools? (Brejzek & Wallen, 2018).

What the actor has never seen- the director never envisaged (Ralph Koltai, 2009).

Scenography is … the art of creating a dynamic space for performance.
(Bob Crowley, 2009).

The multiple ways that performance space generates meanings, referring to how design is performed, and the way staged events are shaped, including the space, text, performers, spectators, and direction (Howard, 2002).

### What is Scenography?

- What kind of sensor will apply in this space?
- Whose data will be collected?
- How does the sensor extract human behavioral data within this space?
- Which company will be interested in these data?
- Dramaturgy In Action

Dramaturgy, in short, is the art of storytelling.

Dramaturgy is a theatrical framework that is assembled through the
configuration of materials. It involves a process, a relationship, a way of
working (Trencsényi, 2015).

Dramaturgy is a rendition from investigation to performativity (JWT, 2026).

Dramaturgy is a creative practice that, through critical observation, curiosity, question, reflect and a process integral to the origination, development, production and reception of plays and theatrical performances with the creative team (Hamilton & Chon, 2022).
